🎓 Academic Viva Defense & Examination Guide

Complete oral defense presentation script, key protocol distinctions, and examiner questions.

Q1: Why is Dijkstra preferred over Distance Vector for emergency V2V messages?
Answer: V2V safety messages have an ultra-low latency threshold ($<100\text{ ms}$). Dijkstra's Link-State algorithm computes the global shortest path instantly in 1 computation step with zero inter-node table exchanges. Distance Vector requires multiple distributed update rounds, risking stale routing and temporary packet drops during highway topology changes.
Q2: How does CRC-16 mathematically guarantee 100% burst error detection?
Answer: CRC uses polynomial Modulo-2 division over $GF(2)$. With generator polynomial $G(x) = x^{16} + x^{12} + x^5 + 1$, any burst error of length $\le 16$ bits cannot produce an error polynomial $E(x)$ divisible by $G(x)$. Therefore, the remainder (syndrome) is non-zero, ensuring $100\%$ detection.
Q3: What is the relationship between Window Size, Delay, and Throughput?
Answer: Throughput scales with window size $W$ according to link utilization $U = \frac{W \cdot T_{\text{tx}}}{T_{\text{tx}} + 2 \cdot T_{\text{prop}}}$. Scaling $W$ from 1 to 16 increased throughput from $38.4\text{ Kbps}$ to $412.8\text{ Kbps}$. However, once $W$ exceeds the Bandwidth-Delay Product (BDP), additional packets buffer in queues, increasing average delay.
Q4: How does AIMD Congestion Control prevent broadcast storms in 200 vehicles?
Answer: When 200 vehicles transmit simultaneously, packet collision and queue overflow occur. Our active AIMD engine monitors queue drop events: upon detecting packet loss, the sending rate is halved ($R = R / 2$), immediately clearing buffer backlog, and then recovered gradually via additive increase ($R = R + 0.1\text{ Mbps}$).
